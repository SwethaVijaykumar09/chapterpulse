Mira found the letter on the bottom step of the lighthouse, damp from the sea air and sealed with black wax. She had climbed those stairs a thousand times, and nothing had ever been left there before. The wax bore the shape of a small bird in flight. Her grandfather had worn that same bird on a ring until the day he vanished. Mira turned the envelope over. Her name was written on the front in handwriting she knew better than her own. She sat down on the cold stone and listened to the waves crash against the rocks below. Then she broke the seal. Inside was a single line: "Do not trust the keeper of the light."
---
By morning the whole village had heard that Mira was asking questions. Old Tomas, who ran the bakery, refused to look at her when she asked about her grandfather. "Some stories are better left at the bottom of the sea," he muttered, wiping flour from his hands. Mira did not give up. She walked to the harbour, where the fishermen mended their nets in silence, and she showed them the little bird on the wax. One by one they turned away. Only a young boy named Eli stayed, staring at the seal with wide eyes. "I have seen that bird before," he whispered. "It is carved on the inside of the lighthouse door, hidden behind the lamp."
---
That night Mira climbed the lighthouse again, her lantern shaking in her grip. The keeper, a tall man named Corvin, was already waiting at the top. "You should not be here," he said softly. Mira stepped past him and pressed her hand against the old wooden door. Behind the great lamp, exactly where Eli had promised, a small bird was carved into the wood. Beneath it was a hidden latch. Her fingers found it, and the wall slid open with a groan. Cold air rushed out of a narrow staircase leading down into the dark. Corvin did not stop her. Instead he smiled. "He has been waiting for you for ten years," he said.
